Exterior Architecture — New Build Villa Parga

The Architecture
in Detail

The primary façades of each Pasha Villa are clad in hand-laid natural Epirus limestone — the same rough-cut stone used in the traditional dry stone walls and village buildings of northwest Greece. Against this, clean white and warm beige plaster framing volumes create contrast. Dark anthracite aluminium frames complete the material palette.

Image 06 · Aerial View — Site Plan

The Complex
from Above

The overhead aerial render shows all six Pasha Villas, their individual private pools (turquoise rectangles), private parking spaces, and the curved access road. The staggered positioning of the homes along the hillside is clearly visible — each villa is offset from its neighbours in both plan and section, ensuring complete privacy between pool terraces. The complex is accessed from a single curved road, with each villa's parking space directly in front of its entrance.

Inside Looking Out —
The Connection

07
Interior · Through the Glass
Floor-to-Ceiling Glass — Pool & Mountains
Taken from inside the ground-floor living area looking directly out through the full-height dark-framed sliding glass doors. The white plaster walls and natural stone floor tiles are in the foreground. The pool is the focal point, with the pergola visible to the right and the Epirus mountains on the horizon at dusk. This perspective shows why the floor-to-ceiling glass system is the defining element of the design: the landscape becomes the living room.
The dark anthracite aluminium frames are double-glazed and thermally broken — standard new-build specification in Greece, maintaining comfortable interior temperatures in both summer and winter.
